About

What this is

EWB Live finds the extreme-weather events of the last 90 days and scores open AI weather models against IFS HRES on them, using ExtremeWeatherBench (EWB) defaults. It refreshes every Monday: events are re-detected each week and scores do not accumulate across runs.

Events

  • Heat wave — ERA5 2 m temperature above the 85th percentile of the Operational WeatherBench ERA5 1990–2019 climatology (per day of year and hour) at every 6-hourly step of a UTC day, over land, tracked into events by the rule set out below.
  • Freeze — the same rule below the 15th percentile. A window with no freeze is a valid result.
  • Atmospheric river — EWB’s AR mask: integrated vapour transport (IVT) of at least 400 kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹ with a ridge criterion. The river must start over the ocean and reach land — making landfall on at least one day; objects entirely over land are excluded, and tropical cyclones are screened out using IBTrACS best tracks.
  • The raw detector map shades each cell by its consecutive completed UTC days meeting the daily rule. Heat shades only cells reaching the detector’s 3-day minimum (heat ramp 3–7+) — below that a cell can never become a heat wave, so it stays blank — while freeze shades from its first completed day (freeze ramp 1–3+) so short cold runs remain visible for context. Today’s in-progress day is dithered as provisional on cells that already qualify; the same daily criterion is the one the detector applies within its AND-chain. The AR layer is EWB detection minus tropical objects (any cell within 15 deg of the equator) and tropical cyclones, dropped whole at the level of the tracked river (no frame-to-frame flicker). A river that briefly dips below threshold for one 6-hour step keeps its id; that step shows its previous footprint with a lighter fill (bridged — display only, the river was not physically detected there).
  • Parts of one system are merged, no two events overlap in time and space, and each type is capped at 15 events.

How a spell becomes an event

Heat waves and freezes are found in two stages. A daily rule picks the pixels: a cell counts on a UTC day when 2 m temperature is past the climatology bound (p85 for heat, p15 for freeze) at all four 6-hourly steps and the cell is on land. A tracker then decides which pixels belong to the same event, and when the event is over — the same numbers for both hazards.

  1. Group the counting cells of the day into regions. A cell belongs to a region when it is counting, or when it sits in a hole the closing fills (up to 12 cells wide) and at least 30 % of the surrounding 13×13 window is counting. Patchy heat inside a broad warm area therefore counts as one object, while a thin scatter of lone cells does not chain across a continent.
  2. Seed a candidate when a region covers 50,000 km² — about 65 cells of the 0.25° grid near the equator, more toward the poles. It has five days to become an event or it is dropped.
  3. Qualify on the first day the candidate's footprint covers 200,000 km². The event keeps its earlier start date, so a heat wave that builds over three days is not cut short. It must last at least 3 days.
  4. Follow it: the event's box grows to cover wherever its footprint moves, up to 8 cells a day, so a heat wave that travels stays one event instead of three. Where two events would claim the same ground, the older one goes first.
  5. End it when its core — the cells that have been counting every day since it started, counted inside the box it had on day 3 — holds less than 100,000 km². Regions that are hotter than the climatology all season (the Sahara, the Amazon, Indonesia) always keep 200,000 km² somewhere, but their day-one heat does not stay put, so those spells close on their own instead of running for a season.

Boxes are reported on 0.25° cell centres. The thresholds live in ewb_live/events/event_tracker.py (Params); the daily masks are read from the same published frames the raw map paints, so the list and the map cannot disagree.

Reproduce the tracker in Python

The rule above in one readable pass, same thresholds, numpy + scipy only: detection.py — run python3 detection.py for a worked example. It leaves out two details of the shipped tracker (events that grow into each other merge; the box can be size-capped), which is why the numbers on this site come from ewb_live/events/. The project's test suite runs this listing against the shipped tracker and fails if the two disagree.

Scoring

Models are scored from daily 00 UTC runs at leads of 0–240 h (the scorecard shows 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 days). Truth is ERA5. ERA5 trails real time by about six days; an event ending after that point would be scored against the IFS HRES 0-h analysis, which favours IFS.

Reading the scorecard

Each cell prints the mean metric over events. Its colour is the relative skill against IFS HRES at the same metric and lead: blue is better, red is worse, whichever way the metric points. Hover or tap a cell for the baseline value, the relative skill and the number of events.

  • RMSE, peak-max MAE, min MAE (K) — lower is better; 0 is perfect.
  • Duration error (h) — signed; 0 is ideal, positive over-predicts. Closeness to zero counts, not sign.
  • CSI — overlap of forecast and observed AR mask, 0–1; higher is better.
  • Spatial displacement (km) — distance between forecast and observed AR; lower is better.
  • Early signal — share of events the forecast flags, 0–1; higher is better.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Heat and freeze are percentile-relative, not absolute hazard levels: a freeze at −43.7 °C over Greenland is ordinary for there.
  • Peak values are ERA5 grid daily-mean temperatures. Station daily maxima run 4–8 °C higher, so figures will not match record-high headlines.
  • Detection uses daily-mean 0.25° fields: heat waves that show only in daily maxima, or that are patchy (parts of Europe in September), can be missed. No event here does not mean no extreme.
  • Cells rest on a handful of events (about 6–15 per type) and the freeze lane is a single event: read it as indicative, not a ranking.
  • ERA5 is also the training reference of several AI models, which gives them a structural advantage over physics-based forecasts; truth inherits ERA5’s own biases.
  • Not every model covers every event: aigfs has no 2 m temperature (no heat or freeze scores) and some models lack some archive dates. Two very large AR events are listed but not scored. Missing cells are shown as — .
